# Campaign assistant guide

## Purpose

This repository tracks the history of a D&D campaign and supports preparation for upcoming sessions. Help the DM maintain continuity, find information, record what actually happened, and develop future possibilities. This is a campaign notebook, not a software project; no build or test workflow is required for ordinary note edits.

## Where to find information

- `1-session.md`: initial players, the relic quest, Ilvanyth and Varun, the party's split, the bard's death, and the paladin freeing the evil god.
- `2-session.md`: roster changes, DM-only truth about the paladin's dragon, the vision explaining the seal, the journey to the silver dragon, encounter preparation and outcomes, and recovery of a body for the bard.
- `3-session.md`: resurrection and its soul cost, Faruk and his family, the city rumors, the stolen meeting letter, and a way through the inner city wall.
- `4-session.md`: arrests and wanted characters, the ruler's murder, the talking sword, the wizards' information, the mysterious ship, and the party's position before the meeting.
- `character_sheets/`: PDF character sheets. Use these for character mechanics, equipment, and any written backstories; use session notes for subsequent events and changes. Read the relevant PDF before claiming a mechanical detail.

Read numbered session files in numeric order. Before preparing a new session, read the latest session and any earlier notes relevant to its hooks. The summaries below reflect sessions 1–4; later session records take precedence for subsequent developments.

## Established history

### Session 1: the relic and betrayal

The original five PCs met a shady quest-giver, Varun, in a bar. He presented a lost relic as a family heirloom and pointed them toward a library. Puzzles there yielded a map leading to a cave guarded by Ilvanyth.

When Varun arrived, the party split: the rogue, paladin, and bard attacked Ilvanyth, while the barbarian and wizard attacked Varun; the wizard tried to save Ilvanyth. Both Varun and Ilvanyth died. The relic imprisoned an ancient evil god. The paladin betrayed his devotion to Bahamut, killed the bard when the bard tried to intervene, and freed the god in exchange for a promise of Bahamut as his pet.

### Session 2: the seal and the silver dragon

The notes describe the paladin riding a pale, chained dragon, a separate true silver dragon carrying something glimmering, and darkness spreading across the world. Ilvanyth contacted the wizard's mind as the cave collapsed and showed the history of the seal: dragons, wizards, and dwarves had allied against Voruzhan; the wizards' and dwarves' pieces were forged with dragon flame. After use, the seal split into two shards, kept in a sacred library protected by deadly puzzles and a dwarven clan mine.

The party followed the silver dragon to save the bard, whose ghost accompanied them. They killed a person who was overburdening a sick horse and freed the horse; Eren's dhampir drank the dead person's blood. An illusion-wall encounter is described, but the notes do not record how the party resolved it. The silver dragon offered relatively little resistance and said resurrection required a body. The party triggered traps in a graveyard, fought zombies, and brought a dug-up body to the dragon.

### Session 3: resurrection and arrival near the city

Berna, Eren, Kudret, and Ekin were absent as players. In the fiction, Berna, Eren, and Kudret performed the resurrection, each losing a small piece of their soul to merge with Ekin's in the new body. They fainted, and Ekin returned as an aasimar cleric. The absent players' characters slept.

The dragon carried the party as far as a proper road. They met Faruk, a 70-year-old who had worked in the mines for the last month. His daughter is a nurse in another city; his son serves in the kingdom's army. The sleeping characters stayed at Faruk's house, except Eren, who was left with a neighbor.

The active party heard that the city ruler was leaving for an important meeting and had a beautiful sword at home, that inner-city wizards suffered illnesses when casting spells, and that disease was spreading. They stole a letter giving the meeting time as 10:30 and learned of a hole in the inner city wall.

### Session 4: murder, theft, and the meeting

Burcu and Eren were absent. Eren remained asleep. Witnesses identified Canberk and Burcu as the letter thieves: Canberk escaped, while Burcu was jailed in the outer city, with questioning by the captain scheduled for the next day.

The reunited active party split between the ruler's house and the barracks. At the barracks, they learned that Voruzhan's release was causing harmful effects from magic, including illness and misfortune. They told the inner-city wizards what they knew and requested three or four soldiers. Later events describe them commanding guards, but the exact number supplied is not recorded.

At the house, Kudret damaged the library looking for a nonexistent secret entrance. Canberk saw assassins in the ruler's room; they left after hearing the noise. The ruler was dead. Canberk took the bloody knife from the corpse, was caught with Hold Person while trying to flee, and had his hands tied. The guards had not yet searched his inventory. Kudret found a talking sword in the attic. The other group used their apparent authority and minor illusions to help Kudret escape and rescue Canberk, who remained wanted. They collected torn library papers.

The papers indicated that a mysterious ship would arrive the next day and appeared bound for somewhere near the ancient library known to the wizard. Berna sneaked into the barracks. Ekin and Cemre gained entry by falsely claiming to attend the important meeting on the dead ruler's behalf. The session ended with them inside the barracks, before the meeting room.

## DM knowledge and information boundaries

- **Explicit DM secrets in session 2:** Voruzhan twisted the paladin's bargain. The chained dragon is mechanical and contains only an essence of Bahamut. The paladin still needs a ritual to capture and enslave Bahamut. These facts were explicitly marked unknown to the PCs; do not reveal them in player-facing recaps unless a later session establishes their discovery.
- Ilvanyth's vision was delivered to Berna's wizard. Do not assume every PC knows its full contents unless sharing is established.
- The silver dragon and the paladin's mechanical dragon are distinct. Varun, the quest-giver, and Voruzhan, the evil god, are also distinct.
- Keep DM truth, individual PC knowledge, party knowledge, rumors, and proposed future events separate. When producing handouts or player recaps, include only information appropriate to that audience.

## Ambiguities to preserve

- `2-session.md` calls the evil god **Voruzhan**; `4-session.md` uses **Varuzhan**. Use Voruzhan provisionally in new notes and recognize Varuzhan as an apparent spelling variant; do not silently rewrite the sources.
- The city ruler is called “Dutch” in the notes. This may mean “Duke,” but the title is unconfirmed. Prefer “city ruler” until clarified.
- Session 3 describes a beautiful sword; session 4 initially calls the theft target a knife, then separately records a bloody knife and a talking sword. Track the knife and sword as separate objects and avoid resolving the wording by invention.
- The city, silver dragon, ancient library, clan mine, and ship have no recorded proper names in these four session files.
- The first session's map library is not established as the same location as the sacred library holding a seal shard.
- D&D edition, current levels, and house rules are not specified by the session notes. Check the sheets and ask the DM when a missing detail materially affects a ruling.

## How to maintain and prepare notes

- Preserve the DM's established events and tone. Make targeted edits; do not rewrite prior sessions or retcon consequences unless requested.
- Continue the root-level `<number>-session.md` naming convention for session records. Clearly label preparation, possible outcomes, and unresolved questions so they cannot be mistaken for played history. If a separate prep file is useful, use an explicit name such as `5-session-prep.md`.
- When recording a played session, capture attendance and character whereabouts, major choices and outcomes, NPC information, item ownership, discoveries and who learned them, unresolved hooks, and the exact stopping point. Only fill fields supported by the DM's account.
- When planning, start with the last scene and unresolved consequences. Offer flexible scenes, NPC motivations, clues, and options that preserve player agency. Label new names, lore, encounters, rewards, and mechanics as proposals until accepted or played.
- The earlier files mix encounter instructions with outcomes. Read conditional wording and DC lists as preparation unless an outcome is recorded; a planned encounter is not proof of what the players did.
- Resolve ordinary organizational choices independently. Ask focused questions only when an ambiguity would materially change canon, a character, or the requested plan; otherwise retain the uncertainty explicitly.
- Cite the relevant session file when explaining campaign facts. If the DM corrects the history, incorporate the correction in the requested scope and keep this guide consistent when affected.
- Do not infer that an absent player left the campaign. Attendance, fictional incapacitation, imprisonment, and permanent departure are different states.
- Keep character sheet PDFs intact unless explicitly asked to alter them. Do not assume their starting inventory reflects later loot or losses.
- Update this guide's roster and current-state snapshot when requested maintenance or new session records make them stale; avoid duplicating every future session here. The numbered records remain the detailed history.
